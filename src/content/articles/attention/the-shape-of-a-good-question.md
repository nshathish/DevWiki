Good questions have a little tension in them. They are specific enough to guide attention, but open enough to reveal something you did not already know.

## Replace verdicts with invitations

“Why is this project failing?” assumes a verdict. “What is making this project harder than it needs to be?” creates an invitation.

The second question does not soften the problem. It gives the problem more than one possible shape.

## Test the edges

Ask what would have to be true for the opposite answer to be useful. This small reversal is often enough to expose an assumption hiding inside the original question.
