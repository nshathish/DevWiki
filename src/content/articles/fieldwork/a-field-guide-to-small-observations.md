Large patterns are usually made from small repetitions. Before looking for a grand explanation, collect a handful of ordinary details.

## Look for friction

Notice where people pause, backtrack, ask for help, or invent a workaround. Friction is a form of testimony. It tells you where the official version of a process differs from the lived one.

## Describe before interpreting

Write down what happened in plain language before deciding what it means. “They opened the menu three times” is more useful than “the navigation was confusing.” The first sentence leaves room for several explanations.

Carry a small notebook, or keep a single running note on your phone. The best tool is the one that lets you record an exact phrase while it is still warm.
