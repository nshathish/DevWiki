Before a project needs a roadmap, it needs a shared sentence. A one-page brief is a place to make the important constraints visible while they are still easy to change.

## Include five things

Write down the outcome, the audience, the constraint, the first proof, and the open question. If a detail does not help someone make a decision, it probably belongs somewhere else.

The “first proof” matters most. It turns an abstract ambition into something the team can put in front of another person and learn from.

## Let it stay unfinished

Leave a visible space for what you do not know yet. A brief that looks complete can quietly discourage better questions.
