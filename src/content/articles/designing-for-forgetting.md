We tend to treat forgetting as a failure of storage. But a mind that remembers everything would be impossible to live inside. Forgetting is not the opposite of attention; it is one of attention’s quietest tools.

## The useful filter

The best systems do not ask us to preserve every detail. They help us decide what deserves a second look. A note should carry enough context to be useful later, without becoming a museum piece that needs its own caretaker.

> Keep the thread, not the whole ball of wool.

Try ending each note with one of three signals: **return**, **share**, or **let go**. The point is not to create another productivity ritual. It is to give your future self a kind exit.

## A smaller archive

An archive becomes generous when it has edges. Keep a short index of ideas you are actively returning to, and let the rest become background. The things worth remembering tend to find their way back through a related question, a familiar phrase, or a lucky coincidence.

Make room for that coincidence.
